\begin{abstract}
We present \BLINCS{}, a $100\%$ stateless hash-based signature scheme based on \BLAKE{} and SPHINCS sized for an operational budget of $q_s \le 2^{32}$ signatures per signing key (128-bit classical single-user security target). Allowing a $128\text{ KiB}$ cached top Merkle heap ($h_{\mathrm{top}} = 12$) and targeting $\le 5.0\text{ s}$ warm $99$th-percentile signing on modern $160\text{ MHz}$ ARM Cortex-M33 hardware wallets (modeled, not measured on Cortex-M hardware), \BLINCS{} achieves fixed-length \textbf{$3{,}292$-byte signatures} ($4{,}392$~B Base64), $32$-byte verifying keys, pure $32$-byte keyed \BLAKE{} everywhere (zero XOF mode), zero static lookup tables required for verification, bounded length-framed application contexts, and \emph{two-stage single-pass salted content hashing}. On a single Apple~M6 core (\texttt{signet}, \texttt{blake3-servil-core 0.11.0}), key loading takes $31.97\text{ ms}$, warm signing takes $4.15\text{ ms}$, and zero-allocation verification takes $39.1\;\mu\text{s}$ ($2{,}276$ compressions at $m = 44, b = 1$).

Beyond the construction, we make three analytical and formal contributions:
(1)~We prove that conditioning honest \UPORS{} signatures on the Octopus sibling cap $\calF = \{S \subset [1024] : |S|=10,\ \mathrm{sibs}(S) \le 44\}$ \emph{breaks negative association} and induces $16.01\times$ adjacent-sibling clustering; using an exact bivariate enumerator ($|\calF| = 256{,}172{,}225{,}164{,}499{,}838{,}464$), a certified $3$-term exponential upper envelope $u_3(t) \ge q_\calC(t)$, $320$-bit Kronecker transforms over exact $1{,}024$-state joint laws, and stratification across all $37$ sibling counts $s \in [8..44]$, we certify raw-query \UPORS{} security of \textbf{$132.13\text{ bits}$ ($99\%$ LCB)} at $q_s = 2^{32}$ (net cap effect $+2.54\text{ b}$, vs.\ $129.63\text{ b}$ exact zero-MC uncapped) and $125.48\text{ bits}$ at $2^{33}$.
(2)~We prove a classical ROM+PRF single-user EUF-CMA reduction with explicit oracle-query accounting, paired with a Lean~4.36.0 formalization (\texttt{formal/Blincs/}, $26$ kernel-checked theorems, zero \texttt{sorry}, zero custom axioms) aligned byte-for-byte with \texttt{SPEC.md}, proving exact \texttt{sliceCount 40 24 468} cardinality via Hockey-Stick and Pascal identities, wire parser round-trip bijectivity, conditional completeness, and a provenance- and target-bound $13$-witness deterministic forgery dichotomy.
(3)~For fair scientific exposition against NIST FIPS~205 \textsf{SLH-DSA-128s} ($7{,}856$~B, $q_s = 2^{64}$), we specify equal-budget $q_s = 2^{64}$ comparison parameterizations (\BLINCSsixfour{}, $5{,}348$~B at identical $16\text{ KiB}$ top cache; \BLINCSsixfours{}, $4{,}962$~B at $d = 7$) that isolate the $31.9$--$36.8\%$ byte savings of \SWOTS{}+\UPORS{} from budget right-sizing, while keeping all production engineering, hardening, and formalization focused on flagship \BLINCS{} ($q_s \le 2^{32}$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Stateless hash-based signatures~\cite{bernstein2015sphincs,bernstein2019sphincsplus,nist2024fips205} rely exclusively on symmetric hash assumptions, avoiding both elliptic-curve and lattice assumptions as well as the catastrophic backup-rollback risks of stateful schemes~\cite{buchmann2011xmss,shrincs2025}. Their primary practical obstacle is signature size: NIST FIPS~205 \textsf{SLH-DSA-128s} requires $7{,}856$~bytes ($10{,}476$~B Base64), while \textsf{SLH-DSA-128f} requires $17{,}088$~bytes.

\BLINCS{} is an experimental stateless hash-based signature suite based on \BLAKE{}~\cite{oconnor2020blake3} and SPHINCS targeting an operational budget of $q_s \le 2^{32}$ signatures per signing key (over $4.29 \times 10^9$ signatures: ten per second nonstop for $13.6$~years) at a \emph{128-bit classical single-user security target} (multi-user security and QROM lifting are explicitly open; see \cref{sec:quantum}). Allowing a $128\text{ KiB}$ cached top Merkle heap ($h_{\mathrm{top}} = 12$) and targeting $\le 5.0\text{ s}$ warm $99$th-percentile signing on modern $160\text{ MHz}$ ARM Cortex-M33 devices (modeled only; no Cortex-M hardware has been measured), \BLINCS{} achieves fixed-length \textbf{$3{,}292$-byte signatures} ($4{,}392$~B Base64).

\paragraph{Summary of contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Joint construction (\SWOTS{} + \UPORS{} Octopus, \cref{sec:construction}):}
    A $4$-layer $12{+}7{+}7{+}7$ hypertree ($h = 33$) pairing constant-sum Sperner \SWOTS{} ($n = 24, w = 40, S = 468$, deterministic $u16$ counter enforcing $z = 8$ leading zero bits) with a single height-$10$ \UPORS{} Octopus tree ($n = 3, w = 24, S = 42, C = 376, k = 10, m_{\max} = 44$) whose leaves fit in one $64$-byte \BLAKE{} block, whose unrankers need \textbf{$0$~B of static lookup tables}, and whose extraction uses pure $32$-byte keyed \BLAKE{} blocks (zero XOF mode).
  \item \textbf{Exact capped-history \UPORS{} security analysis (\cref{sec:upors-security}):}
    We show that enforcing $\mathrm{sibs}(S) \le 44$ on honest signatures breaks negative association~\cite{joagdev1983na} and creates $16.01\times$ sibling co-occurrence, invalidating naive uncapped factorizations and uncapped-signer importance sampling. We resolve this via exact integer enumeration of $\calF$, a certified $3$-term exponential upper envelope $u_3(t) \ge q_\calC(t)$, $320$-bit Kronecker transforms over exact $1{,}024$-entry joint laws $p_{S^*}(A)$, and stratification across all $37$ sibling strata $s \in [8..44]$ (\texttt{tools/upors\_cert.rs}), certifying $132.13\text{ b}$ ($99\%$ LCB) raw-query security at $q_s = 2^{32}$ and $125.48\text{ b}$ at $2^{33}$.
  \item \textbf{Single-user ROM+PRF reduction \& two-stage salted streaming (\cref{sec:security}):}
    We give a single-user classical ROM+PRF EUF-CMA proof with explicit oracle-query accounting, exact chosen-message salt timing, and known-salt degradation analysis, dropping the informal multi-target SPR lifting of earlier drafts.
  \item \textbf{Spec-exact Lean~4.36.0 formalization (\cref{sec:lean}):}
    All six Lean~4 modules match \texttt{SPEC.md} byte-for-byte ($26$ kernel-checked theorems, zero \texttt{sorry}, zero custom axioms), including a direct Hockey-Stick/Pascal proof of \texttt{sliceCount 40 24 468}, total wire parser round-trip bijectivity, and a provenance- and target-bound $13$-witness forgery dichotomy.
  \item \textbf{Equal-budget $q_s = 2^{64}$ ablation \& single-threaded measurements (\cref{sec:comparison}):}
    We introduce \BLINCSsixfour{} ($5{,}348$~B, $16\text{ KiB}$ top cache) and \BLINCSsixfours{} ($4{,}962$~B, $d = 7$) strictly as $q_s = 2^{64}$ scientific comparison baselines against \textsf{SLH-DSA-128s} ($7{,}856$~B), isolating algorithmic savings ($-31.9\%$ to $-36.8\%$) from budget right-sizing, alongside reproducible Apple~M6 measurements and an auditable Cortex-M33 model.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Preliminaries, Primitive, and Notation}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{\BLAKE{} Modes and Single-Block Hot-Path Alignment}
\BLINCS{} invokes only two standard $32$-byte-output \BLAKE{} modes~\cite{oconnor2020blake3} and \emph{never} invokes \BLAKE{}'s extended-output (XOF) mode:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item $\Keyed(K, X) \in \bits^{256}$: \BLAKE{} \texttt{KEYED\_HASH} with $32$-byte key $K$.
  \item $\Derive(\mathsf{ctx}, X) \in \bits^{256}$: \BLAKE{} \texttt{DERIVE\_KEY} with UTF-8 context string $\mathsf{ctx}$ (computing a context-key block once per fixed context string, plus one compression per $64$-byte input block of $X$; for fixed suite contexts, implementations may cache the derived context key).
\end{itemize}
Six pairwise-distinct suite-bound key-derivation contexts derive $K_{\mathrm{sec}}$, $K_{\mathrm{salt}}$, $K_R$, $\mathsf{pk\_seed}$, $K_{\mathrm{pub}}$, and $K_{\mathrm{content}}$ from a $32$-byte secret $\mathsf{seed}$, giving verifying key $\mathsf{SignetId} = \mathsf{pk\_seed} \parallel \mathsf{root}$ ($32$~B).

Every tree, chain, counter-grinding, and message-binding hash is domain-separated by an explicit $16$-byte tweak:
\[
  \Tweak(\mathsf{kind}, \ell, \mathsf{tree}, \mathsf{leaf}, a, b)
  \;=\;
  \mathsf{kind}\;(1) \parallel \ell\;(1) \parallel \mathsf{tree}\;(6) \parallel \mathsf{leaf}\;(2) \parallel a\;(2) \parallel b\;(4),
\]
with all multi-byte integers little-endian and unused fields zero. Hypertree layers use $\ell \in \{0,1,2,3\}$; the single \UPORS{} tree uses $\ell = \mathtt{0x80}$ ($128$). Define $\ShortHash(K, T, X) = \Keyed(K, T \parallel X)[0..16]$. Eight distinct tweak kinds partition all hash calls (\cref{fig:tweak}): every hot-path keyed call---\texttt{CHAIN\_SECRET} ($16$~B), \texttt{CHAIN} ($32$~B), \texttt{ONE\_TIME\_MSG} ($36$~B), \texttt{NODE} ($48$~B), \texttt{FEW\_SELECT} ($52$~B), \texttt{FEW\_CODE} ($52$~B), and \UPORS{} \texttt{ONE\_TIME\_KEY} ($64$~B)---fits inside a \emph{single} $64$-byte \BLAKE{} compression block, and all three counter-indexed sweeps place their incrementing $u32$ counter on a $4$-byte word boundary.

\subsection{Terminology and Notation}
We distinguish the $16$-byte per-imprint randomizer $R$ from the binomial occupancy count $R_{\mathrm{occ}} \sim \mathrm{Bin}(q_s, 2^{-h})$ at a hypertree leaf address; $H_{\mathrm{sel}}$ denotes the raw $32$-byte \texttt{FEW\_SELECT} digest, distinct from the separate indexed \texttt{FEW\_CODE} blocks. In our companion library \texttt{signet}, a signing key is called a \texttt{Signet}, its $32$-byte verifying key a \texttt{SignetId}, and a signature an \texttt{Imprint}; this paper uses standard cryptographic terminology alongside those identifiers.

\section{Design-Space Exploration and the \BLINCS{} Construction}
\label{sec:construction}

\subsection{Structured Design-Space Exploration}
In the Bleichenbacher--Maurer DAG model of hash-chain one-time signatures~\cite{bleichenbacher1996ots} and constant-sum chain-product antichains~\cite{bos1992csum,zhang2023csum}, minimizing signature bytes for a fixed signing work budget balances four coupled levers: hypertree depth $d$ (fewer layers save $386$~B per layer at exponentially higher per-subtree work), top-tree cache size $h_{\mathrm{top}}$ (caching a height-$12$ top tree costs $128\text{ KiB}$ of RAM/Flash and removes $4{,}096$ leaves from warm signing), OTS counter grinding $z$, and few-time tree merging via Octopus~\cite{aumasson2017gravity,abri2025porsfp}.

Our earlier zero-cache draft (\textsf{BLINCS-128-7x5}, $6{,}432$~B, $1\text{ KiB}$ top tree, $7 \times 5$ \textsf{WOTS+} + \textsf{U-FORS}) targeted an uncached $60\text{ MHz}$ Ledger \textsf{ST33} budget ($\le 200\text{k}$ compressions). Re-targeting modern $160\text{ MHz}$ Cortex-M33 devices with a $128\text{ KiB}$ cached top tree raises the warm signing budget to $\approx 450\text{k}$ compressions, enabling $d = 4$ ($12{+}7{+}7{+}7$) and cutting signature size in half to $3{,}292$~B. Within our explored family of single-OTS-type stateless \BLAKE{} schemes at $q_s = 2^{32}$, $128\text{ KiB}$ top tree, and $\le 460\text{k}$ warm compressions, \BLINCS{} sits near the minimum size that clears $128$~bits cleanly without tight-cap tail blowup (whereas tighter-cap candidates trade tail jitter or rely heavily on cap credit).

\subsection{\SWOTS{}: Constant-Sum Sperner Unranking with $z = 8$ Grinding}
For $n = 24$, $w = 40$ (steps $0..39$), consider the exact middle constant-sum slice $\calL_{468}(24, 40) = \{\mathbf{d} \in \{0,\dots,39\}^{24} : \sum_i d_i = 468\}$. Because every vector in $\calL_{468}(24, 40)$ has equal coordinate sum, $\calL_{468}(24, 40)$ is a strict antichain in the chain-product poset $\{0,\dots,39\}^{24}$ (which is Sperner by de~Bruijn--van~Ebbenhorst~Tengbergen--Kruyswijk~\cite{debruijn1951sperner}; note that for dominance only the antichain property is needed, not maximality).

\begin{proposition}[Exact \SWOTS{} Cardinality and Minimality of $w = 40$]
\label{prop:sperner}
Let $N(w, n, s) = |\calL_s(n, w)|$. By inclusion--exclusion over the $n$ upper bounds $d_i < w$ (proved directly on the recursive definition \texttt{sliceCount} in Lean~4, \texttt{sliceCount\_eq\_spernerIE}),
\[
  N(40, 24, 468) = 1{,}973{,}221{,}677{,}906{,}098{,}493{,}567{,}206{,}234{,}001{,}571{,}000 > 2^{120},
\]
whereas $N(39, 24, 456) = 1{,}102{,}272{,}360{,}296{,}506{,}971{,}467{,}046{,}668{,}212{,}509{,}411 < 2^{120}$. Thus $w = 40$ is the exact integer minimum for injecting $120$-bit ranks into $\calL_{S_{\mathrm{mid}}}(24, w)$.
\end{proposition}

At hypertree position $(\ell, \mathsf{tree}, \mathsf{leaf})$ signing child root $\mathsf{node} \in \bits^{128}$, the deterministic signer finds the \emph{smallest} $\mathrm{ctr}_\ell \in \{0,\dots,65535\}$ ($u16$) such that
\[
  H_\ell = \ShortHash(K_{\mathrm{pub}}, \Tweak(\mathtt{ONE\_TIME\_MSG}, \ell, \mathsf{tree}, \mathsf{leaf}, 0, 0),\, \mathsf{node} \parallel \mathrm{u32\_le}(\mathrm{ctr}_\ell))
\]
satisfies $H_\ell[0] = 0$ ($z = 8$ leading zero bits), failing closed without wrapping if all $2^{16}$ values fail (exhaustion probability $(255/256)^{65536} \approx 2^{-370}$ per root in the ideal model). The $15$ trailing bytes $H_\ell[1..16]$ define a $120$-bit rank injected into the first $2^{120}$ lexicographic tuples of $\calL_{468}(24, 40)$.

\paragraph{Effective domain and counter choices.}
Because only the first $2^{120}$ ranks are used alongside $H_\ell[0] = 0$, the effective output domain is exactly $2^{128}$ (the unused $0.570$ bits of $\log_2 N(40, 24, 468) \approx 120.570$ add no collision resistance). For a fixed honest target digest at a fixed address, a fresh raw trial matches with probability $2^{-128}$ (or $2^{-120}$ conditioned on $H_\ell[0] = 0$); testing all $2^{16}$ counters on one other fixed child root hits with probability $\approx 2^{-112}$ at cost $2^{16}$ hashes (unchanged work per hit). Deterministic reuse of the \emph{smallest} valid counter at each physical address $(\ell, \mathsf{tree}, \mathsf{leaf})$ is essential so that signing the same honest child root never leaks two distinct digit vectors. Choosing $z = 8$ over $z = 10$ avoids $z = 10$'s $(1023/1024)^{65536} \approx 2^{-92.4}$ $u16$ overflow hazard across $2^{32}$ signatures while costing only $\approx 256$ hashes per layer ($< 0.3\%$ of signing work). Every layer executes an exact constant $24 \cdot 39 - 468 = 468$ verification chain steps, and stand-alone verifiers unrank $\calL_{468}(24, 40)$ via exact $O(S)$ boxcar deconvolution in $7.5\text{ KiB}$ of stack RAM with \textbf{$0$~B of static lookup tables}.

\subsection{\UPORS{}: Single Height-$10$ Upper-Slice Octopus Tree}
The few-time layer at address $\mathsf{leaf\_addr} \in [2^{33}]$ is a single binary Merkle tree of height $A = 10$ ($L = 1{,}024$ leaves). Each leaf holds $n = 3$ chains of $23$ steps ($w = 24$), so its leaf hash input $\Tweak(\mathtt{ONE\_TIME\_KEY}) \parallel x_{23}^{(0)} \parallel x_{23}^{(1)} \parallel x_{23}^{(2)}$ is $16 + 48 = 64$~bytes (one \BLAKE{} compression block). Codewords lie on the upper constant-sum slice $\calC = \calL_{42}(3, 24) = \{(a,b,c) \in \{0,\dots,23\}^3 : a+b+c = 42\}$ of exact cardinality $|\calC| = 376$ (kernel-checked in Lean~4: \texttt{upors\_slice\_count}, \texttt{uporsCode\_antichain}). Every opened leaf requires an exact constant $3 \cdot 23 - 42 = 27$ verify steps, and $\calL_{42}(3, 24)$'s hexagonal cross-section admits a $10$-line closed-form arithmetic unranker requiring \textbf{$0$~B of static lookup tables}.

\paragraph{Selection, Octopus cap, and unbiased indexed codeword extraction.}
Given $D_{\mathrm{seed}} \in \bits^{256}$ (\cref{sec:content-hash}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{\texttt{FEW\_SELECT} (\texttt{SPEC.md §8.1}):} For $\mathrm{ctr}_{\mathrm{ft}} \in \{0,\dots,2^{32}-1\}$, compute
    \[
      H_{\mathrm{sel}} \;=\; \Keyed\!\big(K_{\mathrm{pub}},\, \Tweak(\mathtt{FEW\_SELECT}, \mathtt{0x80}, 0,0,0,0) \parallel D_{\mathrm{seed}} \parallel \mathrm{u32\_le}(\mathrm{ctr}_{\mathrm{ft}})\big).
    \]
    Scan up to $22$ consecutive $10$-bit chunks starting at bit $0$ (bits $0..220$) to collect the first $k = 10$ distinct leaves in $[1024]$, sort ascending, and compute the exact Octopus sibling count $m = \mathrm{sibs}(S)$. Reject if fewer than $10$ distinct leaves appear (probability $\approx 2^{-49}$ in $22$ draws) or if $m > m_{\max} = 44$. Extract the $33$-bit hypertree address $\mathsf{leaf\_addr}$ from bits $220..253$ (disjoint from bits $0..220$). The deterministic signer picks the smallest valid $\mathrm{ctr}_{\mathrm{ft}}$ (failing closed if $u32$ exhausts); the verifier checks only the encoded $\mathrm{ctr}_{\mathrm{ft}}$.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{FEW\_CODE} (\texttt{SPEC.md §8.2}):} Using indexed blocks $\mathsf{blk} \in \{0, \dots, 65535\}$ ($u16$), compute
    \[
      B_{\mathsf{blk}} \;=\; \Keyed\!\big(K_{\mathrm{pub}},\, \Tweak(\mathtt{FEW\_CODE}, \mathtt{0x80}, 0,0,\mathsf{blk}, 0) \parallel D_{\mathrm{seed}} \parallel \mathrm{u32\_le}(\mathrm{ctr}_{\mathrm{ft}})\big)
    \]
    and read $28$ consecutive $9$-bit chunks, accepting values $< 376$ with zero modulo bias until $10$ codeword indices are collected. In the ideal model, $\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(28j, 376/512) < 10]$ is $\approx 5.9 \times 10^{-6}$ for $j = 1$ block and $\approx 2^{-61}$ for $j = 2$ blocks; both signer and verifier fail closed if $\mathsf{blk}$ would exceed $65535$.
  \item \textbf{Canonical Octopus padding (\texttt{SPEC.md §10.2}):} Emit the $m \le 44$ Octopus siblings bottom-up followed by $(44 - m) \cdot 16$ zero bytes ($704$~B fixed); verifiers MUST check all trailing padding bytes are zero in constant time.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Two-Stage Single-Pass Salted Content Hashing}
\label{sec:content-hash}
To stream arbitrarily large messages $M$ in a single pass while avoiding reliance on unsalted collision resistance of $M$ under normal operation, the signer draws $32$ random bytes $\mathsf{draw}$ \emph{before} reading $M$ and computes (with $\mathsf{framed\_ctx} = \mathrm{u8}(|\mathsf{ctx}|) \parallel \mathsf{ctx}$ for $|\mathsf{ctx}| \le 255$):
\begin{gather*}
  e = \Keyed(K_{\mathrm{salt}}, \mathsf{draw})[0..16], \qquad
  K_{\mathrm{msg}} = \Keyed(K_{\mathrm{content}},\, e \parallel \mathsf{framed\_ctx}), \\
  h_{\mathrm{msg}} = \Keyed(K_{\mathrm{msg}}, M), \qquad
  \mathsf{short} = e \parallel h_{\mathrm{msg}} \;\;(48\text{ B}), \\
  R = \Keyed(K_R,\, \mathsf{short} \parallel \mathsf{framed\_ctx})[0..16], \\
  D_{\mathrm{seed}} = \Keyed\!\big(K_{\mathrm{pub}},\, \Tweak(\mathtt{MESSAGE}) \parallel R \parallel \mathsf{root} \parallel \mathsf{short} \parallel \mathsf{framed\_ctx}\big).
\end{gather*}
Notice that $K_{\mathrm{content}}$ and $K_{\mathrm{msg}}$ are public once $e$ is published: this is a randomized hash family, not a MAC. For honest signing, $\mathsf{draw}$ (and hence $e$) must not be revealed to the message submitter before $M$ and $\mathsf{ctx}$ are committed (streaming APIs must not leak $e$ before the producer commits $M$). If $\mathsf{draw}$ repeats across distinct $(\mathsf{ctx}_1, M_1) \neq (\mathsf{ctx}_2, M_2)$ while $\mathsf{seed}$ remains secret, \texttt{rng\_failure\_\allowbreak hedged\_fallback} proves that either $\mathsf{ctx}_1 = \mathsf{ctx}_2$ with a $256$-bit keyed-\BLAKE{} collision on $h_{\mathrm{msg}}$, or the inputs to the secret-keyed PRF $\Keyed(K_R, \cdot)$ are strictly distinct (preserving pseudorandomness of $R$ up to $128$-bit truncation collisions, while content hashing degrades to $128$-bit known-salt collision resistance).

\subsection{Complete Parameter Summary and Exact Compression Counts}
\Cref{tab:params} summarizes \BLINCS{}. With prederived verifier keys ($K_{\mathrm{pub}}$, $K_{\mathrm{content}}$, $\mathsf{root}$), $|M| \le 64$, $|\mathsf{ctx}| \le 31$, $m \le 44$ consumed Octopus siblings, and $b \ge 1$ \texttt{FEW\_CODE} blocks, verification executes exactly:
\begin{align*}
  &\underbrace{1}_{K_{\mathrm{msg}}} + \underbrace{1}_{h_{\mathrm{msg}}} + \underbrace{2}_{D_{\mathrm{seed}}} + \underbrace{1}_{\mathtt{FEW\_SELECT}} + \underbrace{b}_{\mathtt{FEW\_CODE}}
  + \underbrace{270}_{\text{\UPORS{} chains}} + \underbrace{10}_{\text{\UPORS{} leaves}} + \underbrace{(m + 9)}_{\text{Octopus}} \\

\section{Exact Capped-History \UPORS{} Security Analysis}
\label{sec:upors-security}

This section analyzes the core statistical question of \BLINCS{}: how secure is \UPORS{} when \emph{both} the honest signer and the verifier enforce the Octopus sibling cap $\mathrm{sibs}(S) \le 44$?

\subsection{Exact Enumeration of $\calF$ and Proof That the Cap Breaks Negative Association}
In the ideal-output model of \texttt{FEW\_SELECT} (\texttt{SPEC.md §8.1}), bits $0..220$ (leaf selection) and bits $220..253$ ($\mathsf{leaf\_addr}$) are disjoint and independent. Conditioned on obtaining $10$ distinct leaves in $22$ draws (probability $1 - O(2^{-49})$), the sorted $10$-subset is uniform on $\binom{[1024]}{10}$ by permutation symmetry. Rejecting until $\mathrm{sibs}(S) \le 44$ preserves a uniform $\mathsf{leaf\_addr} \in [2^{33}]$ while changing the honest signer's set distribution to $\mu = \Unif(\calF)$, where $\calF = \{S \subset [1024] : |S| = 10,\ \mathrm{sibs}(S) \le 44\}$.

Define the bivariate subtree enumerator $F_h(x, y) = \sum_{S \subseteq [2^h]} x^{|S|} y^{\mathrm{sibs}_h(S)}$. Clearly $F_0(x, y) = 1 + x$, and combining two independent height-$h$ subtrees adds $1$ sibling iff exactly one subtree is nonempty:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Fh}
  F_{h+1}(x, y) \;=\; 1 \;+\; 2y\,(F_h(x, y) - 1) \;+\; (F_h(x, y) - 1)^2.
\end{equation}
Evaluating \eqref{eq:Fh} in exact $u128$ integer arithmetic (\texttt{tools/upors\_cert.rs}) gives the exact family size and cap acceptance probability with zero Monte Carlo error:
\begin{align*}
  |\calF| &= \sum_{s=0}^{44} [x^{10} y^s]\, F_{10}(x, y) = 256{,}172{,}225{,}164{,}499{,}838{,}464, \\
  p_{\mathrm{cap}} &= \frac{|\calF|}{\binom{1024}{10}} = 7.663726694 \times 10^{-4} \approx 2^{-10.3497}
\end{align*}
(mean $1 / p_{\mathrm{cap}} = 1{,}304.85$ trials; $46.39\%$ of $\calF$ lies at $s = 44$, $25.45\%$ at $s = 43$, $13.66\%$ at $s = 42$).

Because $\calF$ is invariant under binary-tree automorphisms, every individual leaf still has exact marginal inclusion probability $p = 10/1024$. Crucially, however, \emph{conditioning on $\calF$ destroys negative association}~\cite{joagdev1983na}:

\begin{proposition}[Failure of Negative Association under $\Unif(\calF)$]
\label{prop:na-fails}
Under $D \sim \Unif(\calF)$, the leaf indicators $\{\mathbf{1}[\ell \in D]\}_{\ell \in [1024]}$ cannot have nonpositive pairwise covariances; in particular, they are not negatively associated.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $n_j(D)$ be the number of occupied height-$j$ subtrees ($n_0 = 10$, $n_{10} = 1$). Level $j$ emits $2 n_{j+1} - n_j$ siblings, so $\mathrm{sibs}(D) = \sum_{j=0}^{9}(2n_{j+1} - n_j) = \sum_{j=1}^{9} n_j - 8$. Hence $D \in \calF$ implies $\sum_{j=1}^{9} n_j \le 52$. If every distinct pair $u \neq v$ had $\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{1}[u \in D], \mathbf{1}[v \in D]) \le 0$, i.e.\ $\Pr[u, v \in D] \le p^2$, then inside each height-$j$ block every leaf beyond the first contributes at least one same-block pair, giving $\mathbb{E}[n_j] \ge 10 - \frac{1024}{2^j}\binom{2^j}{2} p^2 = 10 - \frac{50(2^j - 1)}{1024}$. Summing just $j = 1,\dots,6$ yields $\mathbb{E}[\sum_{j=1}^6 n_j] \ge 60 - 6000/1024 > 54.14 > 52$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

Extending the integer tree DP \eqref{eq:Fh} to count sets containing fixed pairs $\{0, 2^{d-1}\}$ quantifies the exact pairwise clustering by LCA height $d \in \{1,\dots,10\}$: adjacent siblings ($d = 1$) co-occur \textbf{$16.013\times$} more often than in uncapped sampling ($1.376 \times 10^{-3}$ vs.\ $8.591 \times 10^{-5}$), cousins ($d = 2$) $10.011\times$, $d = 3$ $6.366\times$, $d = 4$ $4.148\times$, $d = 5$ $2.779\times$, $d = 6$ $1.928\times$, dropping below $1\times$ only at $d \in \{9, 10\}$ ($0.847\times$, $0.744\times$).

Furthermore, for a fixed $r = 1$ historical signature $D_1 \sim \Unif(\calF)$, a raw candidate $S^* \sim \Unif(\binom{[1024]}{10})$ covering $D_1$ must satisfy $S^* = D_1$ (which is automatically cap-valid!) and match all $10$ codewords (by the antichain property of $\calC$), giving exact raw-query success probability
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r1}
  P_{\mathrm{raw}}(1) \;=\; \binom{1024}{10}^{-1} \cdot 376^{-10} \;=\; 5.296994457 \times 10^{-50} \;\approx\; 2^{-163.6912},
\end{equation}
with \textbf{zero net cap credit} at $r = 1$ (whereas an uncapped-signer model wrongly credits $p_{\mathrm{cap}}$!).

\subsection{Exact Generating-Function Formulation and Certified $3$-Term Envelope}
Let $q_\calC(t)$ be the probability that a uniform $c^* \in \calC$ dominates the coordinatewise minimum of $t$ i.i.d.\ uniform codewords $c^{(1)}, \dots, c^{(t)} \in \calC$ ($q_\calC(0) = 0$). By $3$-coordinate inclusion--exclusion over $(a^*, b^*, c^*) \in \calC$ (noting no $c \in \calC$ strictly exceeds $c^*$ in all three coordinates simultaneously since $a+b+c = 42$), exact values are $q_\calC(1) = 1/376 \approx 0.002660$, $q_\calC(2) = 0.170004$, $q_\calC(3) = 0.336239$, $q_\calC(4) = 0.458771$, $q_\calC(5) = 0.547735$, with maximum non-unit exponential base $z_{\max} = 371/376 \approx 0.986702$.

Fix an occupancy $R_{\mathrm{occ}} = r \ge 1$ at the target address and a candidate $S^* = \{s_1, \dots, s_{10}\} \in \calF$. Because the $r$ historical sets $D_1, \dots, D_r \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mu = \Unif(\calF)$ are independent, let $p_{S^*}(A) = \Pr_{D \sim \mu}[D \cap S^* = A]$ for $A \subseteq S^*$ ($2^{10} = 1{,}024$ subsets) and define the multilinear probability-generating function $G_{S^*, \mu}(\mathbf{z}) = \sum_{A \subseteq S^*} p_{S^*}(A) \prod_{i \in A} z_i$. Letting $T_i = |\{j \in [r] : s_i \in D_j\}|$, the exact raw-query cover probability at occupancy $r$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:praw}
  P_{\mathrm{raw}}(r) \;=\; p_{\mathrm{cap}} \cdot \mathbb{E}_{S^* \sim \Unif(\calF)}\!\left[\mathbb{E}_{D_1,\dots,D_r \sim \Unif(\calF)}\!\left[\prod_{i=1}^{10} q_\calC(T_i)\right]\right].
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Certified $3$-Term Exponential Upper Envelope]
\label{lem:envelope}
Define $u_3(t) = 1 - \frac{8905}{7144}\,(0.81)^t + \frac{1761}{7144}\,(0.05)^t$. Then $u_3(0) = 0 = q_\calC(0)$, $u_3(1) = \frac{1}{376} = q_\calC(1)$, and $u_3(t) \ge q_\calC(t)$ for \emph{every} integer $t \ge 0$ (with $u_3(2)/q_\calC(2) = 1.0752$, $u_3(3)/q_\calC(3) = 1.0040$, and $1 - u_3(t) = O(0.81^t) \ll (371/376)^t$ for all large $t$). Consequently, for every $S^* \in \calF$ and every $r \ge 1$, with \textbf{zero} negative-association or factorization assumptions,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kronecker}
  \mathbb{E}_{D_1,\dots,D_r \sim \mu}\!\left[\prod_{i=1}^{10} q_\calC(T_i)\right]
  \;\le\;
  \sum_{\mathbf{j} \in \{0,1,2\}^{10}} \left(\prod_{i=1}^{10} c_{j_i}\right) \Big(G_{S^*, \mu}(z_{j_1}, \dots, z_{j_{10}})\Big)^r,
\end{equation}
where $(z_0, z_1, z_2) = (1, \frac{81}{100}, \frac{5}{100})$ and $(c_0, c_1, c_2) = (1, -\frac{8905}{7144}, \frac{1761}{7144})$.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Zero-cancellation evaluation and all-$37$-strata certification (\texttt{tools/upors\_cert.rs}).}
For any fixed $S^* \in \calF$: (i)~an exact $u128$ tree DP computes all $1{,}024$ integer counts $|\calF| \cdot p_{S^*}(A)$ with zero approximation (capturing even $10^{-21}$ full-collision overlaps $A = S^*$); (ii)~a $10$-axis Kronecker transform evaluates $G_{S^*,\mu}(\mathbf{z}_{\mathbf{j}})$ across all $3^{10} = 59{,}049$ tuples in $320$-bit signed fixed-point arithmetic ($256$ fractional bits $\approx 77$ decimal digits, eliminating catastrophic cancellation down to $2^{-256}$), validating \eqref{eq:r1} to $12+$ digits and validating uncapped $r = 2$ ($144.0615\text{ b}$ vs.\ exact closed-form $144.2003\text{ b}$). Because heavily clustered rare targets $S^*$ (e.g.\ $s = 8$) have much higher conditional hit rates, we \emph{stratify} $S^*$ across all $37$ nonempty sibling strata $s \in \{8, \dots, 44\}$ using exact integer stratum weights $w_s = F_{10}[10][s] / |\calF|$, drawing $64$ exact samples per stratum for $s \ge 38$, $32$ for $30 \le s \le 37$, and $16$ for $s < 30$ (via exact top-down $F_h$-guided tree sampling), and forming one-sided $99\%$ confidence bounds in \emph{probability space} (plus exact tail mass for $r > 28$, $< 10^{-30}$) before taking $-\log_2$.

\paragraph{Classical ROM query bound (no free high-occupancy targeting).}
Because $\mathsf{leaf\_addr}$ is extracted from bits $220..253$ of $H_{\mathrm{sel}}$ \emph{jointly} with $S^*$, an offline adversary varying $(e, R, M, \mathrm{ctr}_{\mathrm{ft}})$ cannot pin a high-occupancy address without re-rolling $\mathsf{leaf\_addr}$ on every \texttt{FEW\_SELECT} query. For any fixed signing transcript with empirical occupancy histogram $\{N_r\}_{r \ge 0}$ ($\sum_r N_r = 2^{33}$), a fresh random-oracle \texttt{FEW\_SELECT} query lands on an address of occupancy $r$ with probability $N_r / 2^{33}$, whose expectation over the signing transcript is $\Pr[R_{\mathrm{occ}} = r]$. By linearity of expectation and union bound over $Q_{\mathrm{sel}}$ offline \texttt{FEW\_SELECT} queries, the adversary's expected probability of producing any covered fresh selection is bounded by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rom-upors}
  \Adv^{\mathrm{\UPORS{}}}_{\mathrm{ROM}}(q_s, Q_{\mathrm{sel}})
  \;\le\; Q_{\mathrm{sel}} \cdot \sum_{r = 1}^{q_s} \Pr[R_{\mathrm{occ}} = r]\, P_{\mathrm{raw}}(r)
  \;\le\; Q_{\mathrm{sel}} \cdot 2^{-132.13} \quad (\text{99\% LCB at } q_s \le 2^{32}).
\end{equation}
(Note that at $q_s = 2^{32}$, even the single busiest address across all $2^{33}$ leaves has expected max occupancy $\approx 13$, where $2^{-33} \cdot P_{\mathrm{raw}}(13) \approx 2^{-33 - 99.05} = 2^{-132.05}$.)

\section{Single-User Classical ROM+PRF EUF-CMA Reduction}
\label{sec:security}

We now connect the deterministic Lean~4 witness dichotomy (\cref{sec:lean}) to a clean, explicitly scoped \textbf{classical single-user ROM+PRF} EUF-CMA security bound. We model the keyed public hash family $\Keyed(K_{\mathrm{pub}}, \cdot)$ (over all eight tweak kinds), $\Keyed(K_{\mathrm{content}}, \cdot)$, and $\Keyed(K_{\mathrm{msg}}, \cdot)$ as domain-separated random oracles (justified by distinct keys and injective tweaks proved in \texttt{Tweaks.lean}), and model $\Derive(\cdot, \mathsf{seed})$, $\Keyed(K_{\mathrm{sec}}, \cdot)$, $\Keyed(K_{\mathrm{salt}}, \cdot)$, and $\Keyed(K_R, \cdot)$ as PRFs (so secret chain starts, salts, and randomizers are replaced by independent uniform values in standard PRF hybrids).

\begin{theorem}[Classical Single-User ROM+PRF EUF-CMA Bound for \BLINCS{}]
\label{thm:euf-cma-rom}
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be any classical adversary making at most $q_s \le 2^{32}$ adaptive chosen-message signing queries (where each honest query commits $(\mathsf{ctx}_j, M_j)$ before learning the fresh salt $e_j$) and at most $Q_H$ offline queries to the random-oracle hash functions. Under independent-output PRF hybrids for $\Derive$, $K_{\mathrm{sec}}$, $K_{\mathrm{salt}}$, and $K_R$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:euf-cma-rom}
  \Adv^{\mathrm{EUF\text{-}CMA}}_{\BLINCS}(\mathcal{A})
  \;\le\;&
    \Adv^{\mathrm{PRF}}_{\mathrm{KDF}} + \Adv^{\mathrm{PRF}}_{K_{\mathrm{sec}}} + \Adv^{\mathrm{PRF}}_{K_{\mathrm{salt}}} + \Adv^{\mathrm{PRF}}_{K_R}
    \;+\; \frac{q_s(q_s - 1)}{2^{129}} \;+\; \epsilon_{\mathrm{exhaust}}(q_s) \nonumber \\
  &+\; \underbrace{\frac{Q_H + q_s}{2^{256}}}_{\substack{\text{Events (1)--(3):}\\ 256\text{-bit RO hits}}}
    \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{Q_H}{2^{128}}}_{\substack{\text{Events (4)--(7), (9)--(11):}\\ \text{same-tweak } 128\text{-bit 2PR}}} \nonumber \\
  &+\; \underbrace{\frac{Q_H}{2^{128}}}_{\substack{\text{Events (8), (12):}\\ \text{unrevealed chain}}}
    \;+\; \underbrace{Q_H \cdot \mathbb{E}[P_{\mathrm{raw}}(R_{\mathrm{occ}})]}_{\substack{\text{Event (13):}\\ \text{\UPORS{} cover \eqref{eq:rom-upors}}}}.
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\emph{Step 1 (PRF hybrids and salt/exhaustion aborts):} Replace the six $\Derive(\cdot, \mathsf{seed})$ outputs by independent uniform keys (releasing $\mathsf{pk\_seed}$), replace $\Keyed(K_{\mathrm{sec}}, \cdot)$ by uniform secret chain starts, and replace $\Keyed(K_{\mathrm{salt}}, \cdot)$ and $\Keyed(K_R, \cdot)$ by independent uniform random functions. On $q_s$ fresh signing draws, salt repeats occur with probability at most $q_s(q_s - 1) / 2^{129}$ (note: even on a salt repeat, distinct $(\mathsf{ctx}, M)$ still yields distinct inputs to $K_R$ unless a $256$-bit $h_{\mathrm{msg}}$ collision occurs, per \texttt{rng\_failure\_hedged\_fallback}; we charge $q_s^2 / 2^{129}$ conservatively when bounding Event~(1) under fresh-salt TCR). Honest sampler exhaustion across $q_s$ signatures and $4$ layers has probability $\epsilon_{\mathrm{exhaust}}(q_s) \le 4 q_s \cdot 2^{-370} + q_s \cdot 2^{-4.7 \times 10^6} + q_s \cdot 2^{-10^6}$, which is negligible.

\emph{Step 2 (Deterministic witness extraction):} Conditioned on no honest exhaustion, whenever $\mathcal{A}$ outputs a valid fresh forgery $(\mathsf{ctx}^*, M^*, \sigma^*)$, \texttt{blincs\_forgery\_dichotomy} (\cref{sec:lean}) deterministically produces one of $13$ provenance- and target-bound witnesses:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Events (1)--(3) ($256$-bit digests $h_{\mathrm{msg}}$, $D_{\mathrm{seed}}$, $H_{\mathrm{sel}}$):} Each witness matches an honest $256$-bit random-oracle output on a distinct input (Event~(1) fixes the same $e_q, \mathsf{ctx}_q$ with $M^* \neq M_q$, committed before $e_q$ is revealed). Across $Q_H$ offline queries, each query hits any existing target with probability $\le 1/2^{256}$ (or birthday $\le (Q_H + q_s)^2 / 2^{257}$ if collisions among offline queries are counted; target hits onto the $q_s$ honest digests cost at most $q_s Q_H / 2^{256} \le Q_H / 2^{224}$).
  \item \emph{Events (4)--(7) and (9)--(11) (tweak-addressed $128$-bit second preimages):} Every tweak uniquely encodes $(\mathsf{kind}, \ell, \mathsf{tree}, \mathsf{leaf}, a, b)$. For any single offline RO query $q$, its tweak determines \emph{at most one} honest target output in the entire scheme (whether previously signed or lazily generated along the forgery path: e.g.\ one honest child pair at $(\ell, \mathsf{tree}, \mathsf{lvl}, p)$, one honest leaf endpoint vector at $(\ell, \mathsf{tree}, \mathsf{leaf})$, one honest child root + minimal counter at $(\ell, \mathsf{tree}, \mathsf{leaf})$, or one honest chain node $x_s$ at $(\ell, \mathsf{tree}, \mathsf{leaf}, c, s)$). Because the adversary's input is distinct from that single honest input, a single RO query hits that target with probability exactly $2^{-128}$ (zero multi-target factor in the ROM!). Summing over all $Q_H$ queries yields $\le Q_H / 2^{128}$.
  \item \emph{Events (8) and (12) (unrevealed chain preimages):} In Event~(8), $\mathcal{A}$ outputs the exact honest node $x_{d^*}$ at depth $d^* < d_{\mathrm{honest}}$ along an \SWOTS{} chain; in Event~(12), $\mathcal{A}$ outputs $x_{d^*}$ on a \UPORS{} chain never revealed at any depth $\le d^*$. In both cases $x_{d^*}$ is information-theoretically hidden behind $x_{d^*+1} = H(\Tweak, x_{d^*})$ (or behind the secret chain start when $d^* = 0$, or behind the leaf hash if $d^* = w - 1$ was never revealed). Each offline RO query evaluates at most one candidate preimage against the single honest successor at that tweak, succeeding with probability $\le 2^{-128}$, giving $\le Q_H / 2^{128}$.
  \item \emph{Event (13) (\UPORS{} cover):} Bounded directly by \eqref{eq:rom-upors}: $\le Q_H \cdot 2^{-132.13}$ ($99\%$ LCB at $q_s \le 2^{32}$).
\end{itemize}
Summing the disjoint event bounds gives \eqref{eq:euf-cma-rom}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Honest scope vs.\ standard-model reductions.}
Earlier drafts proposed a standard-model SM-TCR/DM-SPR lifting equation and a ``generous'' preimage-to-2PR theorem with factor $\tfrac12\varepsilon\delta$. We deliberately retract both: (i)~the $\tfrac12\varepsilon\delta$ bound erroneously multiplied marginals without ruling out inversion concentrating on singleton fibers, and (ii)~grinding-selected targets (\texttt{FEW\_SELECT}, \texttt{ONE\_TIME\_MSG}) and correlated tree nodes do not trivially embed into plain uniform-challenge SM-TCR/DM-SPR games without explicit simulations. In the classical ROM+PRF model above, the per-query security exponent at $q_s \le 2^{32}$ is governed cleanly by $\approx 2^{-127}$ per offline hash query (so constant forgery advantage requires $Q_H \approx 2^{127}$ offline queries), while the salt birthday term $q_s^2 / 2^{129} \approx 2^{-65}$ bounds only the negligible event of a repeated salt within $2^{32}$ signatures (on which content hashing falls back cleanly to $128$-bit known-salt collision resistance of $256$-bit keyed \BLAKE{}, not an attack).

  \item \textbf{Wire round-trip bijectivity (\texttt{Scheme.lean}):} Four theorems prove exact wire length (\texttt{encodeImprint\_\allowbreak length} $= 3{,}292$), injectivity (\texttt{encodeImprint\_\allowbreak injective}), parser round-trip (\texttt{decodeImprint\_\allowbreak encodeImprint}, showing $\texttt{decodeImprint}(\texttt{encodeImprint}\ \sigma) = \texttt{some}\ \sigma$), and length rejection (\texttt{decodeImprint\_\allowbreak bad\_len} for any input length $\neq 3{,}292$).
  \item \textbf{Conditional completeness (\texttt{Scheme.lean}):} Theorem \texttt{blincs\_\allowbreak completeness} proves that given valid counters and non-exhausted \texttt{FEW\_CODE}, \texttt{imprintWithCounters} verifies cleanly (including Octopus recovery and canonical trailing zero-padding).
  \item \textbf{Target-bound \& provenance-bound $13$-witness dichotomy (\texttt{Merkle.lean}, \texttt{Wots.lean}, \texttt{Reduction.lean}):} Theorem \texttt{blincs\_forgery\_\allowbreak dichotomy} takes a physical-address-indexed counter table $\texttt{honestCtr} : \mathrm{Fin}\ 4 \to \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N} \to \mathrm{UInt16}$ required \emph{only} along the forged address's $4$-layer path. Every Merkle, Octopus, leaf, and chain collision witness exports the exact honest \texttt{treeNodeAt} or \texttt{chainIter} target on one side, while \texttt{ForgeryWitness.\allowbreak uporsCover} binds $(\mathsf{leafAddr}^*, \mathsf{leaves}^*, \mathsf{codes}^*)$ directly to the fresh parsed forged digests (\texttt{hFreshSeed}, \texttt{hFreshSel}, \texttt{hLeaves}, \texttt{hCodes}, \texttt{hNodup}).
  \item \textbf{Deterministic salt-reuse separation (\texttt{Reduction.lean}):} \texttt{rng\_failure\_\allowbreak hedged\_fallback}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Explicit boundary of the Lean formalization.}
The Lean development is a \emph{deterministic reconstruction, parser, combinatorial, and provenance-bound witness formalization} over arbitrary deterministic \texttt{HashOracle}s; it does not formalize probability distributions, RO query games, PRF hybrids, constant-time execution, or refinement to the optimized Rust boxcar/hexagon implementations. Those probabilistic and implementation layers are established by \cref{sec:upors-security,sec:security} (\texttt{tools/upors\_cert.rs}) and differential KAT/mutation/WASM tests in \texttt{signet}.

\paragraph{Auditable modeled $160\text{ MHz}$ Cortex-M33 budget (not measured on hardware).}
Unlike the desktop super-wave (which allocates $\approx 1.1\text{ MiB}$ transient SIMD buffers), an embedded Cortex-M33 signer streams one leaf at a time via classic $O(h)$-stack Merkle \texttt{TreeHash} ($\approx 1.5\text{ KiB}$ working RAM, plus $7.5\text{ KiB}$ stack for boxcar Sperner unranking and $128\text{ KiB}$ Flash for the precomputed top tree built once at keygen). Warm signing performs $447{,}976$ fixed compressions ($3 \times 123{,}903$ for lower height-$7$ trees $+ 75{,}775$ for the height-$10$ \UPORS{} tree $+ 492$ for the active layer-$3$ \SWOTS{} chains) plus grinding ($4 \times 256 = 1{,}024$ mean / $2{,}570$ $p99$ for \SWOTS{}; $1{,}305$ mean / $6{,}009$ $p99$ for \UPORS{}), totaling $\approx 450{,}305$ mean and $\approx 456{,}000$ joint $p99$ compressions ($p99/\text{mean} = 1.013\times$). At a modeled $1{,}500$ cycles/compression ($9.375\;\mu\text{s}$ at $160\text{ MHz}$, leaving $\approx 18\%$ cycle headroom below the $1{,}754$-cycle ceiling for $5.0\text{ s}$ to cover loop overhead, unranking, and Flash reads), modeled warm $p99$ signing is $\approx 4.27\text{ s}$ (and verify is $\approx 21.3\text{ ms}$). Actual embedded timings depend on compiler codegen, Flash wait states, and DPA/FI countermeasures and remain unmeasured.

\subsection{Quantum Security, Physical CRQC Costs, and Open Problems}
\label{sec:quantum}
\paragraph{Abstract QROM query scales vs.\ realistic physical CRQC costs.}
Our headline $128$-bit target is \emph{classical single-user} security. In abstract quantum random-oracle (QROM) query units, a single $128$-bit preimage target has Grover query scale $O(2^{64})$, arbitrary collisions in a $128$-bit output have BHT query scale $O(2^{128/3})$ (note that arbitrary collisions are not automatically target-bound second preimages or signature forgeries), and known-salt collisions in the $256$-bit content hash have BHT query scale $O(2^{256/3})$. However, as Daniel~J.~Bernstein proved in his landmark SHARCS~2009 cost analysis of quantum hash collisions~\cite{bernstein2009sharcs}, counting abstract oracle queries ignores the physical area--time and quantum-RAM communication costs of a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC) laid out in two- or three-dimensional space: BHT's $O(2^{n/3})$ query algorithm requires $O(2^{n/3})$ coherent memory whose physical routing latency leaves total hardware$\times$time collision cost no better than classical parallel Pollard-$\rho$ search on a realistic 2D mesh, and under fixed-time parallelization across $M$ processors, Grover's $O(\sqrt{N/M})$ depth likewise fails to reduce total hardware$\times$time preimage cost below classical brute force ($M \cdot \sqrt{N/M} = \sqrt{MN}$ grows with $M$, reaching $N$ at $M = N$, and requires massive serial depth for small $M$). Accordingly, while \BLINCS{} is designed with $128$-bit classical target security and relies on no Shor-vulnerable algebraic structure, formal QROM security proofs for adaptive \SWOTS{}/\UPORS{} grinding (cf.~\cite{don2022qrom}) remain open.

\paragraph{Multi-user security and other open problems.}
Multi-user security across $U$ independent keys is not proved in \cref{thm:euf-cma-rom}: because $\mathsf{pk\_seed}$ is $128$~bits, two independent users collide on $\mathsf{pk\_seed}$ (and hence share $K_{\mathrm{pub}}$ across overlapping address domains, since $\mathsf{root}$ is bound into \texttt{MESSAGE} and \texttt{CONTENT} but not into \texttt{CHAIN}/\texttt{NODE}/\texttt{ONE\_TIME\_MSG}) with birthday probability $U(U-1)/2^{129}$. Tight multi-user proofs, QROM lifting, direct comparison against matched-budget \textsf{PORS+FP}~\cite{abri2025porsfp} and \textsf{WOTS+C}~\cite{kudinov2022wotsplusc} implementations, and hardware measurements on physical Cortex-M33 silicon remain open work.
