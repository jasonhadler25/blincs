\begin{abstract}
We present the design, black-box frontier analysis, coding-theoretic foundation, and concrete security analysis of \BLINCS{}, a $100\%$ pure stateless hash-based signature scheme based on BLAKE3 and SPHINCS for a signing budget of $q_s \le 2^{32}$ signatures per signing key. Using a $128\text{ KiB}$ cached top tree ($h_{\mathrm{top}} = 12$) and targeting $\le 5.0\text{ s}$ warm $99$th-percentile signing on modern $160\text{ MHz}$ ARM Cortex-M33 hardware wallets (modeled, not measured on Cortex-M hardware), \BLINCS{} produces fixed-length \textbf{$3{,}292$-byte signatures} ($4{,}392$~B Base64---$2.39\times$ smaller than NIST FIPS~205 \textsf{SLH-DSA-128s} and $5.19\times$ smaller than \textsf{SLH-DSA-128f}), $32$-byte verifying keys, $2{,}267$-compression short-message verification ($39.1\;\mu\text{s}$ on a single Apple~M6 core with zero heap allocations), single-threaded $12{,}288$-chain SIMD super-wave signing ($4.15\text{ ms}$ on one M6 core including self-verification), zero BLAKE3 XOF mode, zero required lookup tables, bounded length-framed application contexts, and \emph{two-stage single-pass salted content hashing}.

Our work makes five contributions. First, we establish hard black-box bounds on stateless BLAKE3 signatures (Bleichenbacher--Maurer OTS DAG bounds, a $3$-way symmetry ceiling $\le 0.585$~bits/chain for $2$-use WOTS, and ARX gate counts ruling out BLAKE3 VOLE-in-the-Head) and map the full hardware-constrained Pareto frontier. Second, we combine two zero-ROM coding-theoretic primitives: (a)~\textbf{\SWOTSZ{}} ($n=24, w=40, S=468, z=8$, $2$-byte counter, $|\mathcal{L}_{468}(24,40)| = 1.973\times 10^{36} > 2^{120}$, $O(S)$ boxcar deconvolution unranking) across a $12+7+7+7$ ($d=4, h=33$) hypertree, and (b)~\textbf{\UPORS{}} ($n=3, w=24, S=42, C=376, A=10, k=10, m_{\max}=44$), a single height-$10$ binary tree with closed-form hexagon unranking, exact single-block $64$-byte BLAKE3 leaf hashing, Octopus path merging with canonical trailing zero-padding, and indexed $32$-byte \texttt{Keyed} codeword derivation. Under elementary without-replacement hypergeometric dynamic programming with \emph{zero} Octopus-cap credit, \UPORS{} achieves \textbf{$128.58$~bits} at $q_s = 2^{32}$; including the verifier-enforced cap yields \textbf{$135.37$~bits} at $2^{32}$ and \textbf{$128.91$~bits} even at $2\times$ overuse ($2^{33}$). Third, we provide a complete declarative formalization in Lean~4.36.0 (zero \texttt{sorry}, zero custom axioms) proving exact \SWOTSZ{} Sperner cardinality, \UPORS{} and \SWOTSZ{} antichain dominance, Octopus completeness and collision extraction, wire bijection ($3{,}292$~B), completeness, and an exhaustive $13$-witness \emph{combinatorial forgery dichotomy}. Fourth, we lift this decomposition into concrete $\EUFCMA$ and attacker-generous keyed-BLAKE3 second-preimage reductions. Fifth, we prove secondary salt-failure resilience (\texttt{rng\_failure\_hedged\_fallback}) and provide a bidirectional theorem-lifting matrix against \SPHINCS{} / FIPS~205.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Stateless hash-based signatures, BLAKE3, SPHINCS+, SLH-DSA, S-WOTS+Z, U-PORS, Octopus multi-proofs, second-preimage resistance, Lean~4 formal verification.

\section{Introduction}

Stateless hash-based signature schemes, pioneered by SPHINCS~\cite{bernstein2015sphincs}, refined in \SPHINCS{}~\cite{bernstein2019sphincsplus,hulsing2022sphincsplus}, and standardized as NIST FIPS~205 (\SLHDSA{})~\cite{nist2024fips205}, achieve post-quantum signatures resting solely on symmetric hash-function assumptions. Standard \SLHDSA{} carries two practical trade-offs at the $128$-bit level: signatures are $7{,}856$~bytes with slow signing (\textsf{SLH-DSA-128s}, $q_s \le 2^{64}$, $h=63$) or $17{,}088$~bytes (\textsf{SLH-DSA-128f}), and hedged signing requires two passes over the message $M$ unless one either drops hedging or hashes $M$ externally without a salt.

\BLINCS{} is an experimental stateless hash-based signature suite based on BLAKE3~\cite{oconnor2020blake3} and SPHINCS for an operational signing budget of $q_s \le 2^{32}$ signatures per signing key (over $4.29 \times 10^9$ signatures: ten per second for $13.6$~years). Allowing a $128\text{ KiB}$ cached top tree ($h_{\mathrm{top}} = 12$) and targeting $\le 5.0\text{ s}$ warm $99$th-percentile signing on modern $160\text{ MHz}$ ARM Cortex-M33 devices (such as Nitrokey~3 or Trezor Safe~5; modeled only, no Cortex-M hardware has been measured), \BLINCS{} shrinks pure stateless BLAKE3 signatures to \textbf{$3{,}292$~bytes}---cutting signature size by $58.1\%$ versus \textsf{SLH-DSA-128s} ($7{,}856$~B) and by $48.8\%$ versus our earlier zero-cache \textsf{U-FORS} draft ($6{,}432$~B)---while verifying short messages in $2{,}267$ BLAKE3 compressions ($39.1\;\mu\text{s}$ on a single Apple~M6 core) and signing in $4.15\text{ ms}$ single-threaded (including self-verification).

\paragraph{Terminology and security-goal scope.}
The companion specification and production Rust crate (\texttt{signet}) use capability terminology---\emph{Signet} ($\SK$), \emph{SignetId} ($\VK$, $32$~B), and \emph{Imprint} ($\sigma$, $3{,}292$~B). Throughout this paper we use standard cryptographic notation $(\SK, \VK, \sigma)$ and refer to $\VK$ as the \emph{verifying key}. Our security analysis follows a two-tier priority:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Primary goal (correct use):} Secret $\mathsf{seed}$, unique unpredictable salt $e$ per signature, and $q_s < 2^{32}$.
  \item \textbf{Secondary goal (incorrect use):} Cheap mitigations for (a) a stuck or leaked salt source repeating $e$ while $\mathsf{seed}$ remains secret, and (b) modest overuse ($q_s \ge 2^{32}$). Compromise of $\mathsf{seed}$ is out of scope.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Preliminaries and Security Definitions}
\label{sec:prelim}

For $m \in \mathbb{N}$, let $\mathcal{B}^m = \{0,\dots,255\}^m$ and $\mathcal{B}^*$ denote finite byte strings. Concatenation is $x \concat y$, slicing is $x[a..b]$ ($x[..n] = x[0..n]$), and fixed-width integers are little-endian. For a finite set $S$, $x \getsr S$ denotes uniform sampling.

\subsection{BLAKE3 Modes (Zero XOF Required)}

BLAKE3~\cite{oconnor2020blake3} is built over a $7$-round ChaCha-like compression function $f_{\mathrm{B3}}$ compressing a $64$-byte block and $32$-byte chaining value into $32$~bytes. \BLINCS{} uses exclusively $32$-byte $\Keyed(K, M)$ (\texttt{KEYED\_HASH}, loading $K \in \mathcal{B}^{32}$ directly into the initial chaining value) and $\Derive(\mathsf{ctx}, M)$ (\texttt{DERIVE\_KEY})---requiring \emph{zero} XOF mode. Any input $M$ of length $\le 64$~bytes executes in exactly one compression $f_{\mathrm{B3}}$.

\subsection{Symmetric Hash and Signature Security Notions}

A context-bound signature scheme $(\KeyGen, \Sign, \Verify)$ operates over contexts $\mathsf{ctx} \in \mathcal{B}^{\le 255}$ and messages $M \in \mathcal{B}^{< 2^{64}}$; $\Adv^{\EUFCMA}_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{A})$ is the probability that $\mathcal{A}$ outputs a valid $(\mathsf{ctx}^*, M^*, \sigma^*)$ with $(\mathsf{ctx}^*, M^*)$ never queried to $\Sign(\SK, \cdot, \cdot)$. Following~\cite{hulsing2016mitigating,bernstein2019sphincsplus,hulsing2022sphincsplus}, we recall $\Adv^{\PRF}_{F}$, $\Adv^{\SPR}_{F}$, Single-Function Multi-Target Target-Collision Resistance $\Adv^{\SMTCR}_{\mathsf{Th}, p}$, Multi-Target Extended Second-Preimage Resistance $\Adv^{\MeSPR}_{\mathrm{content}, q_s}$ (under fresh per-query salt $e_j$), and Decisional Second-Preimage Resistance $\Adv^{\SMDSPR}_{\mathsf{Th}, p}$.

\begin{definition}[Single-Tree Without-Replacement Lattice-Meet $\ITSR$ ($\UPORS{}$)]
\label{def:itsr}
For parameters $(h, A, k, m_{\max})$ and an antichain code $\mathcal{C} \subseteq [w]^n$ ($[w]=\{0,\dots,w-1\}$), let $H_{\mathrm{sel}}$ map $(D_{\mathrm{seed}}, \mathsf{ctr}_{\mathrm{ft}})$ to $(\leafaddr, S, (c_0,\dots,c_{k-1})) \in [2^h] \times \binom{[2^A]}{k} \times \mathcal{C}^k$ subject to the Octopus sibling bound $\mathsf{sibs}(S) \le m_{\max}$ (where $S = \{\ell_0 < \dots < \ell_{k-1}\}$ is strictly sorted). After observing $q_s$ honestly randomized outputs $(\leafaddr_j, S_j, \vec{c}_j)$, adversary $\mathcal{A}$ wins by producing a fresh $(D_{\mathrm{seed}}^*, \mathsf{ctr}_{\mathrm{ft}}^*)$ whose valid output $(\leafaddr^*, S^*, \vec{c}^*)$ satisfies $\mathsf{sibs}(S^*) \le m_{\max}$ and:
\begin{multline}
  \forall\, \iota \in \{0,\dots,k-1\},\;\; \forall\, i \in \{0,\dots,n-1\},\;\;
  \exists\, j \in \{1,\dots,q_s\},\; \exists\, \iota' \in \{0,\dots,k-1\}:\\
  (\leafaddr_j = \leafaddr^*) \land (S_{j,\iota'} = S^*_\iota) \land (c_{j,\iota',i} \le c^*_{\iota,i}).
\label{eq:upors-cover}
\end{multline}
We denote $\mathcal{A}$'s winning probability by $\Adv^{\ITSR}_{\UPORS, q_s}(\mathcal{A})$.
\end{definition}

\section{Black-Box Bounds, \SWOTSZ{}, and Zero-ROM \UPORS{}}
\label{sec:coding}

\subsection{Hard Information-Theoretic Walls for Black-Box Stateless BLAKE3 Signatures}

Before selecting \BLINCS{}, we established four structural bounds showing why pure black-box stateless BLAKE3 signatures at $q_s = 2^{32}$ and $128$-bit security cannot break far below $\approx 3.3\text{ KiB}$ under a $5\text{ s}$ embedded signing budget (\texttt{paper/RESEARCH\_NOTES.md}~\S7):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Bleichenbacher--Maurer OTS DAG bound~\cite{bleichenbacher1996ots}:} Any black-box $1$-time signature with $128$-bit security requires $\ge 241$ hashes/leaf unconditionally and $\ge n \cdot 2^{128/n}$ hashes for $n$ chains ($16n$~B).
  \item \textbf{$2$-use WOTS (\textsf{CUTS}~\cite{deegan2026cuts}) $3$-way symmetry ceiling:} For any marginal distribution on $\{0,\dots,w-1\}$, three i.i.d.\ draws satisfy $\Pr[Y \ge \min(X_1, X_2)] \ge 2/3$ by symmetry of the minimum, capping $2$-use chains at $\le -\log_2(2/3) \approx 0.585$~bits/chain ($\ge 219$ chains $= 3{,}504$~B, strictly worse than $1$-use WOTS $+$ one Merkle level).
  \item \textbf{BLAKE3 VOLE-in-the-Head:} BLAKE3's $32$-bit ARX additions require $\approx 10{,}752$ non-linear AND gates per compression ($8.4\times$ AES-128), yielding $\approx 12\text{--}18\text{ KiB}$ proofs.
  \item \textbf{$16$-byte hash width:} Every Merkle node and chain endpoint must remain $16$~bytes ($128$~bits) against second-preimage and chain-inversion attacks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Pillar 1: Constant-Sum Sperner + Zero-Bit Grinding (\SWOTSZ{})}

By Sperner's theorem on $[w]^n$, the middle constant-sum slice $\mathcal{L}_S(n, w) = \{d \in [w]^n : \sum_i d_i = S\}$ at $S = \lfloor n(w-1)/2 \rfloor$ is the unique maximum antichain~\cite{bos1992csum,zhang2023csum}. In \SWOTSZ{}, we decouple sum enforcement from grinding: a deterministic $16$-bit counter $\mathsf{ctr} \in \{0,\dots,65535\}$ is ground solely for $z = 8$ leading zero bits ($h[0] = 0$) in $h = \ShortHash(\Kpub, T_{\textsf{ONE\_TIME\_MSG}}(\mathsf{ctr}), m)$, and the remaining $120$-bit integer $r = \mathrm{u120\_le}(h[1..16]) < 2^{120}$ is bijectively unranked into $\mathcal{L}_{468}(24, 40)$.

\begin{proposition}[Minimality and Exact Cardinality of \SWOTSZ{}$(24, 40, 468, z{=}8)$]
\label{prop:sperner}
For $n = 24$, $w = 40$ is the smallest Winternitz base with $|\mathcal{L}_{\lfloor 24(w-1)/2 \rfloor}(24, w)| > 2^{120}$:
\begin{multline*}
  |\mathcal{L}_{456}(24, 39)| < 2^{120} < |\mathcal{L}_{468}(24, 40)|\\
  = 1{,}973{,}221{,}677{,}906{,}098{,}493{,}567{,}206{,}234{,}001{,}571{,}000 \approx 2^{120.570}.
\end{multline*}
Both inequalities are mechanically verified by the Lean~4 kernel (\texttt{wots\_\allowbreak sperner\_\allowbreak cardinality}, \texttt{wots\_\allowbreak sperner\_\allowbreak gt\_\allowbreak pow120}, \texttt{wots\_\allowbreak w39\_\allowbreak lt\_\allowbreak pow120}). With $z = 8$, the effective OTS domain is $2^{128.570}$, $\mathsf{ctr}$ overflow probability is $(1 - 2^{-8})^{65536} \approx 2^{-370}$ (eliminating the $2^{-92.4}$ overflow hazard of $z = 10$ on \texttt{u16}), every layer verifies in an exact constant $24 \cdot 39 - 468 = 468$ chain steps, and lexicographic unranking requires $0\text{ B}$ of ROM via $O(S)$ boxcar deconvolution.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Pillar 2: Zero-ROM \UPORS{} (Upper-Layer Lattice Shaping $+$ Single-Tree Octopus)}

Revealing $m$ codewords $c^{(1)},\dots,c^{(m)} \in \mathcal{C} \subseteq [w]^n$ on the same hash-chain leaf exposes their coordinatewise lattice meet $\bigwedge_{j=1}^m c^{(j)} = (\min_j c^{(j)}_0, \dots, \min_j c^{(j)}_{n-1})$. As we showed via systematic testing (\texttt{paper/RESEARCH\_NOTES.md}~\S4), classical distance-thinning codes (Reed--Solomon/MDS, $r$-cover-free families~\cite{kautz1964cff,dyachkov1982cff}, Golay $G_{24}$, Graham--Sloane~\cite{graham1980asymmetric}, Steiner systems, Varshamov--Tenengolts) all lose under salt-randomized uniform queries, while $1$-chain constant-composition leaves hit a universal Shannon ceiling of $\log_2 e \approx 1.4427$~bits/tree.

Instead, \UPORS{} co-locates $n = 3$ equal-length chains ($w = 24$, steps $0..23$, exact $16 + 3\times 16 = 64$~B single-block BLAKE3 leaf hash) on the upper constant-sum slice $\mathcal{L}_{42}(3, 24) = \{(a,b,c) \in \{0,\dots,23\}^3 : a + b + c = 42\}$ of exact cardinality $C = 376$ (kernel-verified in Lean~4: \texttt{upors\_slice\_count}, \texttt{uporsCode\_antichain}). Because $42 > 34.5$ exploits \emph{Lattice-Meet Asymmetry} ($\wedge = \min$ breaks rank symmetry for $m \ge 2$), the exact single-leaf cover probabilities drop to $q_{\mathcal{C}}(1) = 1/376 \approx 0.00266$, $q_{\mathcal{C}}(2) = 0.17000$, $q_{\mathcal{C}}(3) = 0.33624$, while verification work drops to an exact constant $3 \cdot 23 - 42 = 27$ steps per opened leaf. Crucially, the $2$D cross-section of $\mathcal{L}_{42}(3, 24)$ is a symmetric hexagon ($a \in [0..23]$, row width $r(a) = \min(23, 42-a) - \max(0, 19-a) + 1$), admitting a $12$-line closed-form arithmetic unranker requiring \textbf{$0\text{ B}$ of ROM}.

All $2^{10} = 1{,}024$ \UPORS{} leaves live in \emph{one} height-$10$ binary Merkle tree ($A = 10$). For a candidate counter $\mathsf{ctr}_{\mathrm{ft}} \in [0, 2^{32})$, $D_{\mathrm{sel}} = \Keyed(\Kpub, T_{\textsf{FEW\_SELECT}}(\mathsf{ctr}_{\mathrm{ft}}) \concat D_{\mathrm{seed}})$ selects a $33$-bit $\leafaddr$ and $k = 10$ strictly distinct sorted $10$-bit leaves ($S = \{\ell_0 < \dots < \ell_9\}$). Bottom-up Octopus path merging~\cite{aumasson2017gravity,abri2025porsfp} deduplicates shared ancestors across all $10$ leaves; the signer accepts the smallest $\mathsf{ctr}_{\mathrm{ft}}$ with $\mathsf{sibs}(S) \le m_{\max} = 44$ ($p_{\mathrm{cap}} \approx 0.0007325$, mean $1{,}365$, $\mathrm{p99} \approx 6{,}287$ over $2 \times 10^6$ trials; overflow probability $2^{-4.54\times 10^6}$) and zero-pads unused slots to fixed length $44$ (enforced strictly by the verifier). Codeword indices $(u_0,\dots,u_9) \in [376]^{10}$ are sampled by rejection from indexed $32$-byte \texttt{Keyed} blocks ($T_{\textsf{FEW\_CODE}}(\mathsf{blk})$, $28$ $9$-bit draws/block, $99.9996\%$ single block---zero XOF!).

\begin{theorem}[Concrete $\EUFCMA$ Security of \BLINCS{} Under Correct Use]
\label{thm:euf-cma}
For any adversary $\mathcal{A}$ making at most $q_s$ adaptive signing queries against \BLINCS{}, there exist explicit black-box adversaries $\mathcal{B}_1,\dots,\mathcal{B}_{11}$ running in time $\approx t_{\mathcal{A}}$ (plus signing simulation) such that
\begin{align}
  \Adv^{\EUFCMA}_{\BLINCS}(\mathcal{A}) \;\le\;&
    \Adv^{\PRF}_{\Derive}(\mathcal{B}_1)
    + \Adv^{\PRF}_{\Ksec}(\mathcal{B}_2)
    + \Adv^{\PRF}_{\Ksalt}(\mathcal{B}_3)
    + \Adv^{\PRF}_{\KR}(\mathcal{B}_4)
    + \frac{q_s(q_s - 1)}{2^{129}} \nonumber\\
  & + \Adv^{\MeSPR}_{\mathrm{content}, q_s}(\mathcal{B}_5)
    + \Adv^{\SMTCR}_{D_{\mathrm{seed}}, q_s}(\mathcal{B}_6)
    + \Adv^{\SMTCR}_{D_{\mathrm{sel}}, q_s}(\mathcal{B}_7)
    + \Adv^{\SMTCR}_{\mathsf{HT}, p_{\mathrm{ht}}}(\mathcal{B}_8) \nonumber\\
  & + (d + 1)\bigl(\Adv^{\SMDSPR}_{\mathsf{CHAIN}, p_c}(\mathcal{B}_9') + \Adv^{\SMTCR}_{\mathsf{CHAIN}, p_c}(\mathcal{B}_9'') + 24\cdot 2^{-128}\bigr) \nonumber\\
  & + \Adv^{\SMTCR}_{\UPORS, p_{\mathrm{u}}}(\mathcal{B}_{10})
    + \Adv^{\ITSR}_{\UPORS, q_s}(\mathcal{B}_{11}),
\label{eq:euf-cma}
\end{align}
where $d = 4$, $p_{\mathrm{ht}} \le 8{,}960\, q_s$ (including \textsf{ONE\_TIME\_MSG} targets), $p_c \le 429{,}984\, q_s$, and $p_{\mathrm{u}} \le 2{,}048\, q_s$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Exact Without-Replacement DP and Importance-Sampled \UPORS{} \ITSR{}}

Under ideal hashes, $R \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(q_s, 2^{-33})$ prior queries land on any candidate $\leafaddr^*$. Each such query opens a uniformly random $10$-subset of the $1{,}024$ leaves. Conditioned on $R = r$, the probability that a single target leaf is hit by $t \ge 1$ of those $r$ queries is $\Pr[T = t] = \binom{r}{t} (10/1024)^t (1014/1024)^{r-t}$, and the exact without-replacement probability that all $10$ distinct target leaves are hit simultaneously is computed via inclusion-exclusion / exact DP over distinct leaf hits, multiplied by $\bigl(\mathbb{E}[q_{\mathcal{C}}(T) \mid T \ge 1]\bigr)^{10}$ (recovering $+1.5$ to $+3.8$~bits over naive with-replacement approximations!). Crucially, we evaluate BOTH:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Elementary zero-cap-credit DP bound (zero Monte Carlo):} drops the adversary's Octopus constraint $\mathsf{sibs}(S^*) \le 44$ entirely, yielding \textbf{$128.58$~bits} at $q_s = 2^{32}$---clearing $128$~bits purely from elementary combinatorics.
  \item \textbf{True security with verifier-enforced Octopus cap ($m_{\max} = 44$):} verified by $6{,}000 \times 32$ importance-sampling trials (\texttt{paper/RESEARCH\_NOTES.md}~\S8), the cap adds \textbf{$+6.79$~bits} at $2^{32}$ (\textbf{$135.37$~bits}) and \textbf{$+7.35$~bits} at $2^{33}$ (\textbf{$128.91$~bits}), keeping true security above $128$~bits even at $2\times$ overuse!
\end{enumerate}

\section{Attacker-Generous Reduction to Keyed-BLAKE3 Second-Preimage Resistance}
\label{sec:generous}

\begin{lemma}[Preimage-to-Second-Preimage Bridge for $n$-to-$n$ Random Maps]
\label{lem:ow-to-2pr}
Let $f: \mathcal{B}^{16} \to \mathcal{B}^{16}$ satisfy $\Prob_{x \getsr \mathcal{B}^{16}}[|f^{-1}(f(x))| \ge 2] \ge \delta$ ($\delta \to 1 - e^{-1} \approx 0.6321$). If $\mathcal{I}(f(x))$ outputs $x'$ with $f(x') = f(x)$ with probability $\varepsilon$, then $(x, x')$ is a genuine second preimage ($x' \neq x$) with probability at least $\frac{\delta}{2}\,\varepsilon - \Adv^{\SMDSPR} \approx 0.316\,\varepsilon$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Attacker-Generous Reduction to Keyed-BLAKE3 Second-Preimage Resistance]
\label{thm:generous-2pr}
Consider the generous model $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{gen}}$ where chain starts are uniform random $16$-byte strings and $\mathcal{A}$ is handed $(\Ksalt, \KR)$ plus every public intermediate node of every queried subtree. Let $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{nc}}$ be $\mathcal{A}$'s probability of outputting a fresh valid \BLINCS{} forgery satisfying $\neg\textsf{UporsCover}$. Then an explicit reduction $\mathcal{R}$ outputs a concrete keyed-BLAKE3 second preimage $(K, u \neq v, \Keyed(K,u)[..m] = \Keyed(K,v)[..m])$ ($m \in \{16, 32\}$):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Multi-target keyed-BLAKE3 $\DMSPR$/$\SMTCR$ (tight):} across all queried targets with probability $\ge \frac{\delta}{2}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{nc}} - \Adv^{\SMDSPR} \approx 0.316\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{nc}}$ (and $1 \cdot \varepsilon_{\mathrm{coll}}$ on direct collision branches (1)--(7) and (9)--(11)).
  \item \textbf{Single-target plain BLAKE3 $\SPR$:} embedding a single challenge $(K^*, x^*)$ into one uniformly guessed slot wins single-target $\SPR$ with probability $\ge \frac{\delta}{2\, N_{\mathrm{tgt}}}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{nc}} - \frac{\Adv^{\SMDSPR}}{N_{\mathrm{tgt}}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{Secondary Security Analysis: Salt Failure \& Overuse}
\label{sec:rng-fallback}

\begin{theorem}[Two-Stage Salt-Failure Hedged Fallback, \texttt{rng\_failure\_hedged\_fallback}]
\label{thm:rng-fallback}
Suppose the salt source (\texttt{saltseed}) is completely stuck or leaked so the exact same $\mathsf{draw}$ (hence the same salt $e$) is reused across two distinct pairs $(\mathsf{ctx}_1, M_1) \neq (\mathsf{ctx}_2, M_2)$ with $|\mathsf{ctx}_i| \le 255$. Then either:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item $\mathsf{ctx}_1 = \mathsf{ctx}_2$, $M_1 \neq M_2$, and $\Keyed(\Kmsg, M_1) = \Keyed(\Kmsg, M_2)$ ($256$-bit keyed-BLAKE3 collision, $\approx 128$-bit security); or
  \item $\mathsf{short}_1 \concat \framed(\mathsf{ctx}_1) \neq \mathsf{short}_2 \concat \framed(\mathsf{ctx}_2)$, so $R_1, R_2$ remain independent pseudorandom outputs of secret-keyed $\KR$ and \UPORS{}/hypertree hedging stays intact.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

On overuse ($q_s > 2^{32}$), Table~\ref{tab:itsr} shows graceful degradation: \BLINCS{} retains $128.91$ true bits at $2^{33}$ ($2\times$ budget) and $\approx 121.1$ true bits at $2^{34}$ ($4\times$ budget).

\section{Related Work and Open Problems}
\label{sec:related}

Hash-based signatures trace to Lamport~\cite{lamport1979constructing}, Merkle~\cite{merkle1989certified}, XMSS~\cite{buchmann2011xmss}, WOTS+~\cite{hulsing2013wotsplus}, SPHINCS~\cite{bernstein2015sphincs}, \SPHINCS{}~\cite{bernstein2019sphincsplus,hulsing2022sphincsplus}, and FIPS~205~\cite{nist2024fips205}. \SWOTSZ{} unites constant-sum Sperner optimality~\cite{bos1992csum,zhang2023csum,khovratovich2025hypercube} with zero-bit counter grinding~\cite{kudinov2022wotsplusc} while spending $0\%$ of grinding on sum rejection. \UPORS{} builds on Octopus path merging~\cite{aumasson2017gravity} and forced-pruning few-time schemes (\textsf{PORS+FP}~\cite{abri2025porsfp}, \textsf{BPORS+FP}~\cite{ren2026bporsfp}, \textsf{BPORS-C+BFP}~\cite{wang2026bporsc}, \textsf{BGS-CC}~\cite{ren2026bgscc}), while avoiding variable-length signatures, ROM lookup tables, and stateful hybrid rollback risks (\textsf{SHRINCS}~\cite{shrincs2025}). Constrained-device explorations by Project~11~\cite{deegan2026cuts,sphincspe2026} directly informed our cached-top-tree and hardware-wallet modeling. Recent disproofs of beyond-Johnson proximity-gap conjectures~\cite{crites2025proxgaps,dao2026binaryproxgaps,skatharoudis2026sok} reinforce the value of \BLINCS{}'s unconditional exact combinatorial bounds. On implementation assurance, our specification recommends sub-millisecond startup self-tests following Ferguson, Schneier, and Kohno~\cite{ferguson2010cryptography}.

\paragraph{Open problems.}
(1)~Independent human cryptanalysis of our reductions and $128$-bit truncated $7$-round keyed BLAKE3 under $\SMTCR$/$\SMDSPR$; (2)~mechanized probabilistic game-hopping in Lean~4; (3)~full multi-user $\EUFCMA$ proof across $U$ signets sharing $\pkseed \in \mathcal{B}^{16}$; and (4)~formal QROM lifting~\cite{don2022qrom} for \UPORS{}, \SWOTSZ{}, and two-stage content hashing (noting that we do not claim Grover search improves total physical quantum preimage cost over classical brute force).
